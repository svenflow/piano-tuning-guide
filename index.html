Aim at

Green (Guided) means the note came within 1.5 cents of its equal-temperament pitch and held there for half a second; it stays green until it drifts more than 2 cents away. A phone tuner gets each note close, but a tempered 5th is only about 2 cents from pure, so for the 4ths, 5ths and unisons your ear finishes the job: listen for the wobble the step describes.

This note is outside the middle of the piano. A well-tuned piano sits away from equal temperament out here on purpose: a few cents near the middle, up to about 35 cents at the very top and bottom (stretch), so treat the needle as a rough guide and tune the octave by ear.

How to use it

  1. Hold the phone near the strings, in a quiet room. Play one note at a time, medium loud, and let it ring.
  2. Guided aims at the note of the step you are on (or the key you picked). Turn the pin the way the arrow says, a hair at a time, until it locks green. Raw is a plain tuner for any note.
  3. Then do what the step says by ear. The tuner keeps listening while you move between steps; the bar at the top of the card shows the reading.

Phone microphones and the lowest bass notes do not mix well: below about A1 the reading can jump an octave.
